# Healthcare and life sciences

Orientation, last reviewed November 2026. Regulations and programs change: confirm current requirements with the seller's legal or compliance team before making commitments. Do not add statistics unless the user supplies a source.

## Segments behave differently

- **Providers:** hospital systems, academic medical centers, physician groups, clinics, post-acute care. Margin, staffing, patient throughput and clinical quality dominate.
- **Payers:** health insurers and health plans, including Medicare Advantage and Medicaid managed care. Medical cost, member experience, quality ratings and administrative cost dominate.
- **Life sciences:** pharmaceutical, biotech and medical device companies. Time to market, regulatory approval, trial execution and commercial launch dominate.

Ask which segment before giving advice. A hospital CFO and a pharma commercial leader share almost nothing.

## Buyers and what they are measured on

- **Provider CFO / VP of finance:** operating margin, cost per case, revenue cycle performance (denials, days in accounts receivable).
- **CIO / CTO / CISO:** EHR stability, security, integration burden, uptime, vendor count.
- **CMO / CMIO / CNO (clinical leaders):** patient safety, outcomes, clinician time and burnout, adoption by clinicians.
- **VP of revenue cycle:** denials, clean claim rate, collections, coding accuracy.
- **Supply chain / value analysis:** product cost, standardization, contract compliance.
- **Payer leaders:** medical loss ratio, Star ratings (Medicare Advantage), member retention, prior authorization workload.
- **Life sciences leaders:** clinical trial timelines and enrolment, regulatory submissions, field force effectiveness, launch performance.

## How deals get approved (typical, varies by organization)

1. A clinical or operational sponsor identifies the problem.
2. Product and supply purchases often go through a **value analysis committee** that weighs clinical evidence and cost.
3. Technology purchases go through **IT and security review**, including a security questionnaire and integration review with the EHR (for example Epic or Oracle Health).
4. If the vendor will create, receive, store or transmit protected health information, a **business associate agreement (BAA)** is required.
5. Many hospitals buy through **group purchasing organizations (GPOs)** such as Vizient, Premier or HealthTrust. Ask whether the seller has, or needs, a GPO contract.
6. Finance and, for larger deals, executive or board approval. Budgets are often set annually; fiscal years vary by organization.

Life sciences adds **quality and validation** steps for systems used in regulated work, and **medical, legal and regulatory (MLR) review** for any promotional content.

## Rules that shape the deal

- **HIPAA (Privacy and Security Rules) and HITECH:** govern protected health information. Question for the seller: will the product touch PHI, and can you sign a BAA?
- **Interoperability standards (HL7, FHIR):** question: how does the product integrate with the EHR, and who does the integration work?
- **FDA regulation:** applies to drugs, devices and some software (software as a medical device). Question: does the product make clinical claims that could make it a regulated device?
- **21 CFR Part 11 and GxP:** electronic records and validated systems in life sciences. Question: is the system used in regulated processes, and what validation documentation exists?
- **Open Payments (Sunshine Act):** reporting of payments from manufacturers to clinicians. Question for life sciences sellers: does the product touch HCP engagement or spend?
- **Anti-Kickback Statute and Stark Law:** limit inducements tied to referrals. Sellers should not offer anything that looks like a referral incentive.

## Words that build credibility (use correctly)

Throughput, length of stay, readmissions, denials, clean claim rate, revenue cycle, value analysis committee, BAA, PHI, EHR integration, clinician adoption, care pathway, Star ratings, MLR (medical loss ratio for payers; medical-legal-regulatory review in pharma — know which one is meant).

## Outsider phrases to avoid

- "Patients are your customers" (say patients; many clinicians dislike "customers").
- "We're HIPAA certified" (there is no official HIPAA certification; say what safeguards exist and that you sign BAAs, if true).
- "It's plug and play with Epic" (unless proven; say what integration exists and who does the work).
- Leading with AI or technology before the clinical or financial problem.

## Common objections

- "We're in the middle of an EHR upgrade / freeze." Ask when the window opens and what the evaluation process looks like for the next one.
- "Clinicians won't adopt another tool." Ask what made past tools succeed or fail; show how the product fits existing workflow.
- "Security will never approve this." Ask what the review requires; offer documentation (for example a SOC 2 report or HITRUST certification, only if the seller has one).
- "We'd buy this through our GPO." Ask which GPO and whether an agreement already covers the category.
- "Show me the evidence." Ask what evidence the committee will accept; do not overstate studies.

## Questions that show understanding

1. Which committees or reviews would a purchase like this go through here?
2. How are you measured on this problem this year, and what changed recently?
3. Who on the clinical side would need to champion this for it to be adopted?
4. Will this touch PHI, and what does your security review usually ask for?
5. Is there a GPO contract or an existing vendor in this category?
