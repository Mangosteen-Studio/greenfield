# Email types and templates

Placeholders are in `[brackets]`. Replace every one from the call notes, or leave it as `[confirm: …]`. Never fill a placeholder with an invented detail.

## 1. Same-day recap (after a discovery or first call)

Send the same day, while the buyer still remembers the conversation.

```text
Subject: [Their company] + [Your company]: recap and next step

Hi [Name],

Thanks for the time today. Here's what I heard, so you can correct anything I got wrong:

- [Their priority, in their words]
- [What makes it hard today]
- [What a good outcome looks like, and by when]

I'll send [what you promised] by [day].

Next step: [specific step] on [day, date] with [who].

Did I miss anything?

[Your name]
```

## 2. Demo follow-up

Tie what you showed to the questions they asked. Do not re-pitch features they did not react to.

```text
Subject: [Workflow you showed] for [their team]: follow-up

Hi [Name],

Thanks for walking through [workflow] with us today. The parts that mattered most to your team:

- [Their question or reaction] → [your answer, or a link to the exact clip/page]
- [Their question or reaction] → [answer]

One open item: [question you could not answer]. I'll have it to you by [day].

Next step: [step] on [day, date] with [who].

[Your name]
```

## 3. Executive recap

Senior leaders read the first two lines. Lead with the outcome they care about.

```text
Subject: [Outcome they named]: next step

Hi [Name],

Thank you for the time today. You said [their priority, in their words] matters most by [timeframe they gave].

We agreed [next step] with [who] on [date]. [Champion's name] and I will bring [what you will bring].

[Your name]
```

## 4. Forwardable champion summary

Written so the contact can forward it to people who were not on the call. No internal jargon, no pricing unless the buyer asked for it.

```text
Subject: Summary for [stakeholder or team]: [problem] at [their company]

Hi [Name],

Here's a short version you can forward to [stakeholder]:

The problem: [one sentence, in the buyer's words]
Why now: [the trigger or deadline they mentioned]
What we would test: [the specific thing a next step would prove]
What we need from [stakeholder]: [one concrete ask]

Happy to adjust anything before you send it.

[Your name]
```

## 5. No-show reschedule

Assume good intent. Make rescheduling one click.

```text
Subject: Missed you today: new time?

Hi [Name],

Looks like today didn't work out. No problem. Two options that could work:

- [Day, date, time]
- [Day, date, time]

If the priority has shifted, just tell me and I'll check back in [timeframe].

[Your name]
```

## 6. No-reply follow-up

Each follow-up adds one new, relevant thing: an answer you owed, a resource tied to what they said, or a simpler next step. Reply in the original thread so the context travels with it.

```text
Subject: Re: [original subject]

Hi [Name],

One thing I said I'd find out: [answer or resource tied to what they said on the call].

Still aiming for [next step] on [date]. Does that still work, or has something changed on your side?

[Your name]
```

Plan up to three follow-ups, spaced a few business days apart and then longer. The last one should make it easy to say no: “If now isn't the right time, tell me and I'll close this out.” Then stop.
