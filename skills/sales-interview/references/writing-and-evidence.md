# Writing and evidence guardrails

Adapted from Greenfield's VOICE, CRITIC, VERIFICATION and QUALITY_GATES, and POV's signal/business-consequence discipline. These rules are bundled here so this skill works independently. Interview preparation uses its own intake and evidence labels, not Greenfield's stage sequence or outreach gates.

## Remove AI slop before delivery

Avoid in authored copy: leverage, utilize, comprehensive, robust, strategic alignment, holistic, synergy, cutting-edge, best-in-class, seamless integration, drive value, empower, delve, it's worth noting, in today's landscape, in the current environment, unlock potential, transformation journey, meet you where you are, game-changing, revolutionary, and unquantified at scale. Accurate attributed quotations may retain source wording; don't adopt it as the candidate's voice.

A phrase scan alone is insufficient. Apply these checks:
- **Company-swap test:** If replacing the company name leaves the recommendation unchanged, add the actual evidence and decision it changes, or delete the sentence.
- **Consequence test:** Explain what a signal means for a named function or buyer. A funding announcement alone is not a buying need or budget allocation.
- **Action test:** Replace “build relationships with key stakeholders” with a specific role, question, purpose and next action. Don't invent a name to sound specific.
- **Speak-aloud test:** The candidate should be able to say and explain it naturally. Prefer concrete verbs, short connected paragraphs and ordinary language. Avoid repeated rhetorical triplets, stacked fragments, forced contrasts (“not X, but Y”), press-release claims, grand openings and vague intensifiers.
- **Decision test:** Replace lists of sales-framework acronyms with the decision, evidence and tradeoff. Use a framework when the assignment requires it or it clarifies the work.
- **Ownership test:** Never insert invented experience, inflated personal credit or flattery about the hiring company. Match the candidate's voice without manufacturing expertise.

Example (fictional assignment, not a real claim):
Weak: “Leverage a robust platform to unlock operational efficiency.”
Better: “The brief says reconciliation delays month-end close. I would ask the controller which step causes the delay before proposing automation.”

## Evidence checks

Keep assignment fact, researched fact, candidate-provided, inference, unknown and simulation-only separate. A source's assertion is not proof that its claim is true. Check that the cited passage supports the actual claim; label vendor-reported outcomes accordingly. Use primary sources for current executives, strategy and capabilities. Record source date or access date without confusing them.

Never invent quotes, savings, ROI, urgency, relationship strength, panel scoring criteria or product behavior. Show formulas and labeled inputs for illustrative economics; do not present the result as a forecast. Public executive priorities do not prove that a target account has budget or wants the product.

If a claim can't be established with a reasonable targeted check, stop repeating searches: mark the gap, give a validation action and continue with supported preparation. Fictional assignments can support an exercise POV without a public-news trigger.

## Final editorial check

Review the substantive draft for specificity, evidence, commercial logic, human voice, time fit and audience separation. Revise the offending passage, not merely the banned word. Make up to two focused revision passes; if a substantive gap remains, remove the unsupported assertion or label the hypothesis and report what remains unresolved. Never release a known false claim because a retry count expired.

Do not issue a decorative “9/10,” “QC passed” or interview-success prediction. State meaningful limitations plainly. Feedback during rehearsal must cite what the candidate actually said or missed.
