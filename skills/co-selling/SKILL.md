# Co-Selling

An AI-guided partner-led sales skill by Yousuf Imran, Mangosteen Studio.

Help a seller turn a potential partnership into a specific customer opportunity with a reason for both companies to act, clear responsibilities, and a credible path to customer value. Support both directions equally: a partner selling our product, and us selling theirs. In a reciprocal relationship, evaluate each direction separately; one viable motion does not prove the other.

Invoke with `$co-selling` or say “Run Co-Selling with [partner] for [product or account].” This workflow also works when pasted into another capable assistant.

## Start from the actual decision

Reuse information already supplied. First identify the user's seat and starting point. Do not assume the user already knows which partner to work with. If unclear, ask: “Are you selling from inside a hyperscaler, selling a product with or through a hyperscaler, or building a co-sell plan for a specific customer company?”

| Starting point | Central question | Primary output |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyperscaler seller → partner ecosystem** | Which partners can help my territory or customers, and how do I get the right ones engaged? | Detailed co-sell plan with partner signals, shortlist, marketplace economics, and activation through delivery |
| **Product seller → hyperscaler or platform partner** | Why would this platform's sellers help sell our product, and how do we work an opportunity together? | Detailed co-sell plan with platform signals, field engagement, customer benefits, and transaction through delivery |
| **Named customer → co-sell plan** | For this company, which combination of sellers and partners can solve the problem and advance the sale? | Account-specific Co-Sell Brief and joint action plan |

Read the selected path in [references/entry-paths.md](references/entry-paths.md). The third path is an account-level lens and can follow either of the first two; retain the user's seat when switching to it. Confirm whether a named company is the user's employer, a prospective partner, or the end customer if context does not establish that. A plan for recruiting a partner company and a plan for selling into a customer company are different deliverables.

For both seller paths, the default final output is a detailed, evidence-backed co-sell plan, not just a shortlist or strategy summary. Include a short executive decision followed by the operational detail in [references/co-sell-brief.md](references/co-sell-brief.md). Shorten only when the user requests a quick assessment or narrower deliverable. Lack of evidence calls for explicit gaps and validation work, not invented detail.

## Research the distribution path and partner signals

Read [references/distribution-and-marketplaces.md](references/distribution-and-marketplaces.md) to choose and verify the route. Prioritize AWS, Microsoft, and Google Cloud as the initial hyperscaler set for this workflow, not as an asserted market-share ranking. Consider Salesforce and relevant SaaS, data, and AI infrastructure ecosystems when their customer footprint and capabilities fit. Respect the user's chosen partner; assess its route and constraints before suggesting alternatives. Treat services companies primarily as access, implementation, operation, and solution partners unless an actual resale or procurement arrangement is established.

Separate the company whose field sellers create access from the marketplace that processes the transaction and the platform on which the product runs. They may be three different parties. Explain the path from customer need to seller participation to purchase, activation, adoption, and renewal.

Read [references/partner-signals.md](references/partner-signals.md) and perform the signal research before choosing plays or finalizing either seller plan. Signals from the hyperscaler or chosen platform partner are a required part of the plan, alongside customer and product-partner signals. Show how each consequential signal changes partner selection, seller messaging, timing, or the next action. Public ecosystem enthusiasm alone cannot establish account-team interest.

For other partner-led situations, retain the general workflow: identify our company and product, the partner and its offer if known, and the sales direction. Then establish whether the user is choosing partners, activating a relationship, or working a named deal.

Ask the next question that changes the decision, normally one at a time during discovery. Do not force a full interview when the user has provided enough material or requested a quick assessment. Build a working brief as evidence arrives. Keep moving with labeled gaps where they do not prevent useful work.

Choose the appropriate scope:

- **Partner fit:** Evaluate a candidate or compare a shortlist. Deliver a fit assessment, disqualifiers, and a small validation experiment.
- **Partner activation:** There is a relationship but little selling activity. Identify the field sellers, incentives, first accounts, enablement, and a bounded pilot.
- **Joint account plan:** Match a partner's demonstrated access and capabilities to specific customer needs. Prioritize a few actionable accounts.
- **Live deal:** Resolve the customer case, relationship ownership, sales choreography, commercial route, and next customer commitment.

Do not require a partner program to pursue one deal. Do not invent named accounts when none are available; start with an account-selection hypothesis and the evidence needed to test it.

## Establish the motion and roles

Classify the motion by what each company actually does. These can overlap:

| Motion | Selling arrangement | Question to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Referral | One company introduces a buyer; the other sells and contracts | What makes an introduction qualified, accepted, and attributable? |
| Joint selling | Both contribute to the sales process; contracts may be separate | Who leads the customer interaction and each decision? |
| Resale / distribution | A company sells another supplier's offer through its commercial channel | Who quotes, contracts, bills, renews, and supports it? |
| Services-led | An adviser, agency, or integrator recommends or delivers the product | What delivery work and customer outcome justify involvement? |
| Embedded / OEM | The product becomes part of another company's offer | What packaging, technical, support, and commercial dependencies exist? |
| Marketplace-assisted | A marketplace provides a transaction route | Does it change procurement, and who actually creates demand? |

Record explicitly: product supplier, relationship owner, opportunity lead, contracting entity, delivery owner, support owner, and renewal owner. Mark unconfirmed roles as proposed. A partner badge, integration, listing, or shared customer logo is not evidence of an active co-sell relationship.

For direction-specific questions and commercial mechanics, read [references/motion-playbook.md](references/motion-playbook.md) only for the relevant motion.

## 1. Prove the customer reason

Start with the customer's problem, buyer, trigger, and desired outcome. Explain why involving this partner improves access, confidence, implementation, purchasing, or results for this particular customer.

Distinguish a combined solution from two adjacent products. Identify dependencies and gaps: what is available today, what has been tested together, what requires services, and what is merely proposed. Consider whether a direct sale or another route would serve the customer better.

Produce a short joint value hypothesis:

> For [customer / segment] facing [specific problem], [our contribution] and [partner contribution] can produce [outcome], because [mechanism and evidence]. We still need to validate [critical assumption].

Use customer language. Do not substitute “strategic alignment” for a business case or imply measured results where only a hypothesis exists.

## 2. Prove the partner and seller reason

Assess three separate cases:

- **Our company:** Revenue or retention opportunity, access gained, cost to sell and serve, and what control or margin we give up.
- **Partner company:** Product revenue, service revenue, retention, differentiation, or customer outcome that makes the effort worthwhile.
- **Individual seller:** Quota or compensation treatment, relevance to their current accounts, effort required, credit risk, and what they can do next.

Find the partner sponsor, field seller, and delivery or technical counterpart. An alliance leader's interest does not establish field commitment. Look for actions: account nomination, an accepted introduction, joint discovery, a technical resource, or an agreed customer next step.

Assess fit using evidence, without arbitrary weighted scores: customer overlap, complementary capability, current access, seller motivation, delivery capacity, economics, and competing interests. Treat missing information as unknown rather than a failed criterion. Name the weakest assumption and how to test it.

## 3. Map accounts and relationships

For each candidate account, capture:

| Account / entity | Customer need and timing | Our relationship | Partner relationship and last meaningful interaction | Joint play | Evidence / confidence | Next action and owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Distinguish customer, prospect, active opportunity, and former customer on each side. Record relationship strength through demonstrated access, not just contact presence. A LinkedIn connection or CRM match is a possible path, not permission or willingness to introduce.

Prioritize accounts where customer need, relevant partner access, and a willing seller intersect. A large overlap list alone is not a pipeline. For a live deal, map the customer champion, decision maker, technical evaluator, procurement, and blockers to an owner on our side or the partner's side.

Use only account data available for this purpose. Prepare a minimal shareable subset; do not assume permission to disclose a whole CRM export, customer pricing, or internal notes to a partner.

## 4. Agree how the sale works

Complete the marketplace mechanics and customer-benefit checks in [references/distribution-and-marketplaces.md](references/distribution-and-marketplaces.md) whenever the proposed route involves a marketplace or commitment benefit. Verify the exact offer, customer agreement, purchasing entity, billing route, timing, and eligibility. Distinguish spend commitment drawdown, prepaid balances, promotional credits, product usage credits, and seller quota credit. Never translate all of these into “burn your credits.”

Draft a working agreement for the opportunity, sized to the motion:

- Who introduces whom, and what context the introducer will provide.
- Who leads discovery, the demo, technical validation, proposal, negotiation, and close.
- Which offer each party owns, who can approve pricing, and who contracts and bills.
- How opportunity registration, exclusivity, territory, existing direct opportunities, competing partners, and sales credit are handled when relevant.
- Who implements, handles escalation, supports the customer, and owns renewal or expansion.
- Which customer information can be shared, with whom, and through what channel.

Use owner, action, dependency, target date, and evidence of completion for the joint action plan. Label partner and customer actions **proposed** until accepted. Avoid competing outreach and duplicated discovery. Keep a single coordinated next customer step.

Unknown commercial terms should generate a validation action, not an invented commission, discount, entitlement, or legal conclusion. Consult current official program documentation when the task depends on a named partner program's rules; record the source and date. Planning terms are proposals, not executed agreements.

## 5. Make the partner able to sell

Build the minimum enablement needed for the next opportunity:

- Buyer, problem, and trigger the seller should recognize.
- A brief explanation of the joint offer and the partner's role.
- Qualification questions and clear reasons to disqualify.
- Current proof, product boundaries, and demo or technical support path.
- A precise handoff: required context, receiving owner, and proposed response expectation.
- Answers to likely customer and partner objections, including overlap with existing offers.

For activation, prefer working through a real account with a seller over treating training attendance as success. When messaging is requested, draft the appropriate partner recruitment note, seller activation ask, introduction, or joint customer follow-up. Tie it to evidence and a small concrete ask. Do not imply the partner has endorsed an unapproved draft.

## 6. Decide whether to advance

Use a recommendation with reasons and missing evidence:

- **PURSUE:** There is a credible customer problem, useful partner contribution, willing owners, and a workable next step. Identify unresolved commercial dependencies before commitments are made.
- **VALIDATE:** The opportunity is plausible but access, motivation, capability, or economics needs testing. Specify the smallest experiment and what would change the decision.
- **PARK:** Timing or capacity prevents progress. Give a specific reopening trigger.
- **DECLINE:** Evidence shows a material conflict, poor customer fit, unworkable economics, or unsupported delivery promises. Explain the decisive reason.

Also state the next action's readiness: ready to approach the partner, ready for a joint customer meeting, or ready for a commercial proposal. These require different evidence; do not treat a promising partner as a qualified deal.

For a pilot, define a scope, named owners, review date, success evidence, and stop or redesign condition. Agree numerical targets from the situation; do not present arbitrary pipeline targets as benchmarks. Track progression from nominated accounts to accepted introductions, discovery, qualified opportunities, wins, and successful delivery. Include time and cost where known.

Distinguish partner-sourced from partner-influenced opportunities using explicit working definitions. Count each opportunity once in total pipeline even if both labels apply. Bookings, collected revenue, gross margin, and partner payouts are different measures.

## Deliver the Co-Sell Brief

Use [references/co-sell-brief.md](references/co-sell-brief.md) for the detailed plan by default in both seller paths. Write substantive findings, decisions, and actions in each relevant section; do not deliver an empty template. For an explicitly quick request, deliver only the relevant sections. Preserve unknowns instead of filling the template with invented detail.

Every completed assessment should leave the user with:

1. A recommendation and the evidence behind it.
2. A defined sales direction and named or proposed responsibilities.
3. The customer case and both companies' incentives, including the field seller's.
4. The best account or validation experiment and its next action.
5. The material risks, evidence gaps, and who can resolve them.

## Evidence and execution discipline

Attribute consequential facts to the user, a supplied artifact, or a source with a date. Distinguish confirmed facts, inferences with their basis, and unknowns. Preserve conflicting evidence rather than silently choosing a convenient version. Never invent partner status, customer consent, relationships, certifications, integrations, commercial terms, or product capabilities.

Use available research and connected systems when useful, without implying access to absent tools. This skill produces plans and drafts. Sending messages, sharing account data, registering deals, changing CRM records, or making commercial commitments requires authorization for that action; respect authorization already given.

Before finalizing, check that platform/partner signals are sourced and connected to actions, distribution and transaction routes are explicit, customer benefits have their eligibility conditions, and both companies' field seller incentives are addressed. Confirm the recommended next step follows from the evidence, sales directions have not been conflated, and every immediate action has an owner or an explicit ownership gap. State remaining limitations plainly. If interrupted, save the current decision, evidence, unresolved questions, and next action so discovery can resume without repetition.
