# Detailed Co-Sell Plan

Default deliverable for both the hyperscaler seller and the product seller going through a hyperscaler or chosen platform. Populate with findings and decisions, not instructions telling the user to do all the analysis. Begin with a concise executive decision, then provide operational detail. Scale account and partner counts to scope; retain depth for the selected plays. A named customer plan uses the same structure at account level.

Do not omit important sections merely because evidence is unavailable. State what is unknown, the decision it affects, and the exact verification action and owner. Omit sections that are genuinely inapplicable with a brief reason. If the user requests a quick scan, narrow accordingly.

## 1. Executive decision and scope

- Recommendation: PURSUE / VALIDATE / PARK / DECLINE, with decisive evidence.
- User's company, role, product, territory/segment, and desired outcome.
- Starting path and selected hyperscaler/platform/distribution partner.
- Customer entity or account-selection scope; time horizon.
- Best joint play, strongest supporting signal, biggest risk, and immediate next move.
- Readiness for partner engagement, customer meeting, and commercial proposal stated separately.

## 2. Distribution strategy

Explain why the selected route fits the customer footprint, product, access, and economics. Respect a user-selected platform; show concerns and alternatives without silently replacing it.

| Route | Customer/product fit | Field access | Technical fit | Transaction readiness | Potential benefit and conditions | Effort/conflict | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Map relationship channel, supplier, hosting platform, marketplace/reseller, contracting/billing entities, and delivery/support owners. Identify where parties differ and what that changes. For a chosen route, compare only material alternatives such as direct sale, alternate marketplace, or channel private offer.

## 3. Platform and partner signal intelligence

Use the ledger from partner-signals.md: source, event/effective date, checked date, fact/inference status, relevance, counterevidence, and action. Cover the chosen platform's strategy, applicable program/commercial signals, partner capabilities and willingness, and customer/account-team signals.

Explain each priority signal through this chain: observed fact → customer/use-case fit → partner contribution → field seller benefit → precise ask. Include negative evidence and what would change the recommendation. Distinguish current, expired, inferred, and internal-only information.

## 4. Partner selection and ecosystem coverage

For a hyperscaler seller, provide the researched candidate set, justified shortlist, capability coverage, and activation priority. For a product seller, map the platform's account and specialist teams plus any complementary partners needed. For a named account, distinguish incumbent, candidate, recommended, and committed parties.

| Partner/team | Specific contribution | Evidence and customer access | Field counterpart | Capacity/status | Conflict or gap | Priority and next validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Explain why each recommended participant is needed and why relevant alternatives were deprioritized. Do not insert an SI or reseller merely to make the ecosystem map look complete.

## 5. Prioritized customers and buying triggers

| Customer/entity | Problem and trigger | Current footprint | Our access | Partner access and evidence | Buyer/stage | Joint play | Priority reason | Next action/owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

If no accounts are available, provide a concrete selection profile and validation plan, not invented company matches. Separate relationship overlap from confirmed buyer interest and qualified opportunities.

## 6. Joint solution and customer value

For each priority play, describe the problem, buyer, current alternative, our capability, each partner's contribution, and expected outcome with proof. Explain any claimed gap in the platform's native offer using current evidence.

Include deployment/integration dependencies, data and security requirements material to the purchase, implementation effort, limitations, and acceptance criteria. Label unmeasured outcomes as hypotheses. State whether the customer would still want the solution without the marketplace benefit.

## 7. Incentives for each company and seller

| Party | Business objective | Individual seller benefit and evidence | Work/cost taken on | Commitment demonstrated | Ask or gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For a hyperscaler seller, include customer outcomes, relevant workload/adoption contribution, actual applicable seller incentives, and the product partner's reason to invest. For a product seller, include platform-specific relevance, what we will do to make the seller's work easier, our economics, and the customer's purchasing reason. Separate alliance sponsorship from field execution.

## 8. Stakeholder and access plan

Map the customer champion, economic buyer, technical evaluator, procurement/FinOps owner, and blockers. Map our AE/SE, the platform AE/specialist/partner contact, transaction support, and delivery counterparts as relevant.

| Person or role gap | Organization | Responsibility/influence | Evidence of relationship | Internal owner | Approach and proposed ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Identify the best credible introduction path and who can authorize or carry it. Do not interpret contact data as willingness to introduce.

## 9. Marketplace and program readiness

| Requirement | Current evidence/status | Applies to which offer/customer/route | Gap | Owner and next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover applicable program participation, co-sell eligibility, offer/listing status, technical requirements, geographic availability, buyer purchasing account, acceptance permissions, and any registration or referral workflow. Distinguish required prerequisites from optional incentives. Cite current official sources and confirm customer-specific conditions.

## 10. Customer benefits and commitment analysis

Populate the benefit ledger from distribution-and-marketplaces.md. Assess simplified billing, committed-spend drawdown, usable balances or credits, procurement steps saved, accountability, and solution value independently. For each, state confirmed/conditional/unavailable/unknown, supporting evidence, and exact language the seller can use.

Record the actual commitment or credit instrument, agreement period, remaining amount, other forecast spend, eligible product charges, rate/cap, purchase and recognition timing, and verification owner. Show calculations when supported; otherwise supply the missing-input model. Compare marketplace versus direct purchase and keep cash cost, commitment contribution, and potential savings separate.

## 11. Offer, procurement, and transaction execution

Describe the selected flow from proposal to acceptance, billing, entitlement, activation, and benefit verification. Include public/private/channel offer as applicable, contracting party, price authority, term, payment schedule, buyer account/entity, procurement/security/legal steps still needed, and who verifies charges count as expected.

| Step | Owner | Required input/dependency | Target date | Completion evidence | Confirmed/proposed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Address deal registration, sales credit, existing direct opportunities, channel conflicts, renewals, and permitted account-data sharing. For a submission-based benefit, include the actual submission and validation process and deadlines. Do not promise approval before the provider's required validation.

## 12. Field engagement and seller enablement

Provide two substantive internal talk tracks as part of the plan: our case to the chosen partner/platform seller, and the customer-facing joint value explanation. These are planning/enablement copy, not sent outreach.

Include the relevant signal, customer/use case, why this partner, proof, work we own, help requested, and next step. Equip the seller with discovery questions, qualification/disqualification criteria, demo support, handoff requirements, and responses to objections about native overlap, existing partners, procurement, unsupported credit claims, and delivery accountability.

Draft emails or messages when requested; do not make communication drafting a prerequisite for completing the plan.

## 13. Joint action plan and customer milestones

| Workstream/action | Account/play | Our owner | Partner owner | Customer counterpart | Dependency | Target date | Evidence of progress | Accepted/proposed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Connect partner validation and seller engagement to discovery, technical proof, customer decision, procurement, close, and adoption. Choose a single coordinated next customer step. Name ownership gaps instead of assigning unaccepted obligations as commitments.

## 14. 30/60/90-day execution and first-week focus

Adapt to the actual sales cycle and time horizon. Detail near-term validation and activation, subsequent customer/opportunity execution, and adoption or expansion milestones where realistic. Do not forecast a close by day 90 merely to fill a schedule.

For the first week, identify the highest-impact actions, owners, resources, dependencies, and outputs. Include a proposed review cadence, decision makers, and escalation path. Pilot success and stop/redesign conditions should be explicit and evidence-based.

## 15. Delivery, support, and renewal

Define implementation scope, acceptance criteria, responsible teams, support/SLA boundaries, escalation lead, adoption measures, renewal owner, and expansion conditions. If the plan promises one accountable owner, identify the scope and evidence supporting that claim. Separate consolidated invoicing from operational accountability.

## 16. Economics and measurement

Show seller economics using known costs and revenue, with fee/payout assumptions and sensitivities. Define partner-sourced and influenced labels and count an opportunity once in total pipeline. Track qualified partner engagement, introductions, discovery, qualified opportunities, wins, activation/adoption, cycle time, and realized economics as relevant. Label baselines and targets as known, proposed, or unavailable.

Track actual commitment recognition or credit application separately from product bookings and workload consumption. Include a benefit-verification owner after purchase, not just a predicted benefit before close.

## 17. Risks, disproof, and alternative actions

| Risk/counter-signal | Evidence | Consequence | Owner | Mitigation or validation | Stop/reconsider trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Include material customer-fit, native competition, relationship, incentive, technical, delivery, procurement, eligibility, and economics risks. Explain what would make direct purchase or another partner better. Do not bury evidence that contradicts the recommended route.

## 18. Sources, open decisions, and next move

Provide the source index with checked dates, conflicting or unverified claims, and a short list of decisions the user needs to resolve. Each critical gap needs a verification action and owner or role. End with the single most useful next move and distinguish a completed plan from a validated partnership or qualified customer deal.
