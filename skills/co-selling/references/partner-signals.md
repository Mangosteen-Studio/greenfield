# Partner signals: evidence to sales action

Use for every detailed seller plan. Research the hyperscaler or chosen platform, candidate product partners, and target customers. The aim is to identify where a partner's priorities meet a customer problem and a seller willing to act.

## Collect the operating context first

Reuse the user's materials. Establish the seller's seat, territory, product and use case, preferred distribution partner, current relationships, marketplace listings, open opportunities, and known customer purchasing routes. Ask for missing internal context incrementally; do not demand confidential contracts or entire account exports when a relevant clause or confirmed field is sufficient.

For partner choice, compare only relevant alternatives and explain the criteria. For a partner already selected by the user, research its incentives and constraints in depth. A weak fit should result in an explicit concern or validation plan, not silently changing their chosen platform.

## Research four layers

| Layer | Signals to investigate | Source and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Platform strategy | Prioritized workloads, industries, migrations, AI initiatives, launches, regional investments, ecosystem calls | Official announcements, product docs, earnings statements, partner resources; shows a strategic hypothesis, not account-team demand |
| Program and commercial | Changes to co-sell rules, eligible offers, field incentives, funding, private offers, procurement routes, commitment treatment | Current official program docs plus authorized program/account confirmation; identify who and what actually qualifies |
| Partner capability and willingness | Relevant releases, integrations, customer proof, practice investments, implementation capacity, sales coverage, accepted account nominations | First-party evidence and direct partner context; separate capability from willingness and capacity |
| Customer and field execution | Current project, buying trigger, renewal, cloud agreement period, budget owner, procurement preference, seller request, solution gap, accepted introduction | Supplied account notes and appropriate direct confirmation; strongest basis for a named account play |

Also research counter-signals: a new native feature that weakens our differentiation, a competing partner entrenched in the account, unsupported deployment geography, partner delivery constraints, expired incentives, exhausted commitment allowance, customer preference for direct purchasing, or recent rejected introductions.

A missing native feature is a hypothesis until checked against current product documentation and the customer's requirements. Do not assert “the hyperscaler does not have this” simply because a partner sells it.

## Source and prioritize

Use current first-party sources for provider rules. Use partner directories and marketplaces for candidate discovery, then verify claims on the underlying provider, product, and customer pages. Capture the event/effective date separately from publication date and research date. Recent publishing does not make an old announcement a new trigger.

Prioritize signals by relevance to the customer problem, evidence specificity, recency appropriate to the event, and actionability. A general keynote theme is context. A relevant account seller asking for a partner solution is a stronger actionable signal. Do not inflate confidence through multiple articles repeating one announcement.

For every consequential signal, record:

| ID | Organization and signal | Event / effective date | Source and checked date | Fact / inference / unknown | Customer and seller relevance | Counterevidence / expiry | Action and owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then build the reasoning chain: **observed signal → customer/use-case match → partner contribution → platform and field seller benefit → precise ask → evidence needed next**. Label inference at the step where it occurs.

## Turn signals into both sellers' plans

For the hyperscaler seller:

- Translate platform priorities and account needs into capabilities to source from partners.
- Use candidate evidence to select the product and delivery partners for each play.
- Explain how the proposed solution helps the customer and the seller's verified objectives.
- Identify the counterpart, enablement gap, customer opportunity, and ask for partner participation.

For the product seller:

- Identify which platform priorities the product can substantively support.
- Establish the workload or business outcome mechanism, rather than merely saying the product runs on the platform.
- Find the account seller and specialist for the relevant customer/use case, plus the alliance contact that can help navigate.
- Make a specific contribution and ask: discovery participation, technical validation, an appropriate introduction, or procurement confirmation.

Example, explicitly hypothetical: a platform announces an industry AI initiative. This is a reason to investigate whether our verified governance capability helps that initiative. It is not proof of funding or a willing account seller. The next move is to validate the customer requirement and ask the relevant specialist to assess a named use case.

## Research completion

The plan should identify the strongest actionable signals, the relevant negative signals, and which parts remain internal-only or unverified. If none are found, say so and design discovery; do not decorate the plan with unrelated news. No fixed signal count substitutes for coverage of the actual decision.

Attach a source index and a refresh trigger for time-sensitive claims. A change to program eligibility, offer status, contract period, or customer procurement route should trigger revalidation before the corresponding benefit is used in a proposal.
