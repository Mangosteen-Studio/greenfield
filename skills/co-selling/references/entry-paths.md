# Three entry paths

Use the section matching the user's starting point, then apply the shared customer case, incentives, ownership, evidence, and decision workflow in SKILL.md. These paths guide discovery and change the emphasis; they are not three mandatory stages. Both seller paths culminate in the detailed Co-Sell Plan in co-sell-brief.md, including partner signals, distribution mechanics, qualified customer benefits, economics, field engagement, and execution through delivery. Read partner-signals.md for signal research and distribution-and-marketplaces.md for route mechanics. Apply the product-seller path to a selected SaaS, data, or AI platform as well as a hyperscaler; use its actual program and commercial model.

## 1. Hyperscaler seller: discover and activate partners

Start with the seller's territory, segment, geography, customer priorities, and the commercial or adoption outcomes they own. Establish whether they are an account seller, specialist, or partner-facing seller. Do not assume their quota or credit rules.

### Discover the partners the work requires

Translate customer problems into needed capabilities before choosing logos. Consider software vendors, systems integrators, regional specialists, managed service providers, advisers, and transaction partners where relevant. Separate partners that create access, provide a product, deliver implementation, operate the solution, or facilitate purchasing.

Build candidates from available internal partner records, the user's account-team knowledge, current official partner directories, and first-party capability or customer evidence. If research access is unavailable, produce the search criteria and evidence gaps; do not fabricate a discovered shortlist. Do not limit discovery to partners already named by the user unless requested.

Compare candidates on:

- Relevant solution capability and supporting customer evidence.
- Industry and geographic fit, including delivery coverage.
- Verified relationship with the customer or target segment.
- Named field counterpart and willingness to work this opportunity.
- Technical and delivery capacity, with current availability still to confirm.
- Contribution to the customer's outcome and the hyperscaler's relevant objective.
- Conflicts with other partners, existing account commitments, or customer preferences.

A directory listing establishes only what that listing supports. It does not prove account access, current capacity, seller commitment, or superior fit. Distinguish a broad candidate list from a qualified shortlist.

### Turn discovery into coverage and activation

Use a matrix: **customer need / account cluster → required capability → candidate partner → evidence → field counterpart → gap → next validation action**. Recommend a manageable shortlist justified by the territory, rather than adding partners solely to maximize coverage counts.

For each priority partner, identify one account or use-case hypothesis, the appropriate partner seller, the hyperscaler account owner, their respective incentives, and the first joint action. Where multiple partners are needed, explain each one's distinct role and who coordinates delivery. Surface competitive alternatives without suggesting that rival partners exchange sensitive account or pricing information.

**Deliver:** a detailed Co-Sell Plan containing the partner shortlist and rationale, signal-to-action analysis, coverage gaps, account plays, marketplace and customer-benefit qualification, seller engagement, commercial mechanics, economics, joint action plan, and delivery/renewal responsibilities. Progress is demonstrated partner participation and customer movement, not the number of ecosystem meetings.

Example request: “I'm a hyperscaler AE covering manufacturers. Help me find the right software and implementation partners for my territory.”

## 2. Product seller: sell with or through a hyperscaler

Start with our product, target buyer, cloud architecture and dependencies, target hyperscaler, existing relationships, and any actual partner-program or marketplace status. Separate supplied status from independently verified facts.

Distinguish three questions:

1. **Customer case:** Why does this product help this customer, and why does the hyperscaler belong in the solution?
2. **Field seller case:** What account objective could our product help the hyperscaler seller achieve, what evidence supports that, and what effort are we asking them to make?
3. **Commercial route:** How will the customer purchase, and what program or transaction prerequisites actually apply?

Potential contributions such as cloud adoption, migration, workload growth, retention, or faster implementation are hypotheses until supported for this product and account. Show the mechanism. Do not invent consumption estimates, field compensation, program benefits, or quota credit. A product hosted on a cloud is not automatically a compelling seller proposition.

Map the account seller, relevant technical specialist, partner-facing contact, and transaction support role as needed. Identify names through available evidence; otherwise use role gaps. A partner-manager relationship does not replace access to the account team.

Assess readiness separately for technical fit, program participation, transaction readiness, and field engagement. A marketplace listing does not establish co-sell eligibility or an engaged seller. Selling through a marketplace does not necessarily mean the hyperscaler resells the product. Do not assume a purchase qualifies against customer cloud commitments, incentives, or funding.

When these details affect the recommended action, verify current requirements in official documentation for the named provider and record the date. Use authorized internal confirmation for account-specific eligibility and commercial treatment. Do not block early customer or partner discovery merely because an optional transaction route is not yet established.

Choose a first joint account or a concrete account-selection hypothesis. Prepare a field engagement brief: customer problem, product contribution, hyperscaler relevance, current deal evidence, work we own, exact help requested, and a proposed next customer step.

**Deliver:** a detailed Co-Sell Plan containing platform signal research, distribution choice, account/team map, customer and field seller value cases, offer and program readiness, marketplace transaction flow, benefit and commitment analysis, economics, joint execution, and delivery/renewal responsibilities. Keep program administration proportional to what that action requires.

Example request: “We sell a data product and want AWS sellers to work opportunities with us. Where do we start?”

## 3. Named customer: build the co-sell plan

Start with the exact customer entity, our seller's seat and offer, the customer problem and buying stage, existing cloud or vendor footprint if known, and current relationships. A named customer need not already have an active opportunity.

Map the ecosystem around this account: customer decision makers, our account team, any hyperscaler account team, incumbent software vendors, implementation or managed service partners, and procurement channel. Include only relevant parties; a hyperscaler is optional in this path.

Evaluate partners against this customer's needs and access, using the discovery method in Path 1 if needed. Distinguish incumbent, candidate, recommended, and committed partners. The largest partner or the strongest global alliance may not be the best local account fit.

Develop a specific joint play with the customer outcome, each party's contribution, evidence, technical dependencies, buyer access, purchasing route, and delivery responsibilities. If comparing alternative partner combinations, explain tradeoffs before choosing the proposed team.

Create one coordinated customer plan: who earns the introduction, leads discovery, proves the solution, handles each objection, proposes terms, and delivers after signature. Record the customer decision being sought and the evidence that will show progress. Do not substitute an internal partner meeting for a customer milestone.

**Deliver:** the Co-Sell Brief for the named customer, selected or proposed partner team and why, stakeholder map, joint action plan, and next customer commitment to seek. If evidence is thin, deliver an account discovery plan and explain what is needed before recommending a commercial push.

Example request: “Build a co-sell plan for Acme. We sell the application, their cloud team is involved, and we need an implementation partner.”
