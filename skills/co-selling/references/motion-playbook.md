# Direction and motion playbook

Read the sections relevant to the selected motion. Use these as decision prompts, not a mandatory questionnaire.

## A partner sells our product

- What do their customers already buy from them, and where does our product solve a problem they encounter?
- Is their contribution introduction, sales execution, transaction, implementation, or several of these?
- Why would their seller make room for this offer? Confirm compensation or credit treatment; do not infer it from company-level economics.
- Can they qualify and explain the product accurately? What do they need us to own?
- Who responds to their first referral? What context is sufficient to accept or decline it?
- What makes them fear being bypassed, losing account control, or inheriting support work? Define an appropriate handoff and communication plan.
- Are resale rights, pricing authority, delivery capacity, and support obligations established where needed?

The immediate deliverable is a partner-ready play: customer signal, opening problem, qualifying questions, proof, disqualifiers, our support, and the first named account or test.

## We sell a partner's product

- What customer need does their product address that our current offer cannot satisfy?
- Why is carrying their product better for this customer than referring elsewhere or declining the work?
- What have we verified about capabilities, implementation, security requirements, and support?
- Who on our team will learn, position, and support the offer, and what do they stop doing to make time?
- Are we referring, reselling, delivering services, or bundling? Which obligations and customer promises do we take on?
- Can we get supplier help during discovery, demos, technical evaluation, escalation, and renewal?
- Do the economics still work after enablement, sales engineering, delivery, support, and collection costs?
- Where could the offer compete with our own product, confuse our position, or put customer trust at risk?

The immediate deliverable is an internal go/no-go case plus a customer qualification and supplier handoff plan. A commission alone is not a customer reason.

## Joint or reciprocal selling

Define a separate opportunity hypothesis for each direction, even when a shared sponsor coordinates both. Do not promise equal lead volume or reciprocal introductions without evidence and agreement. For one combined customer deal, distinguish each product's scope, dependencies, price authority, and contracting route.

Choose a customer-facing lead and a coordinated discovery agenda. Establish whether the customer wants one accountable delivery party or accepts separate suppliers. Identify what happens if one part of the solution is delayed or not purchased.

## Commercial mechanics by motion

| Motion | Mechanics to resolve before the relevant commitment |
|---|---|
| Referral | Qualified referral definition, acceptance, existing-account exclusions, attribution window, payout trigger and basis, follow-up ownership |
| Resale | Right to sell, territory, quote authority, supplier price, resale price, discounts, billing, collections, cancellations, renewal, support |
| Services-led | Product versus services scope, implementation capacity, handoff, acceptance, escalation, ongoing maintenance |
| Embedded / OEM | Packaging, licensing, deployment dependencies, version compatibility, branding, support chain, roadmap assumptions |
| Marketplace | Current eligibility and transaction rules, listing or private-offer status, fees, procurement requirements, seller involvement |

Use the actual agreement or current official documentation for program-specific claims. Missing terms are dependencies. This table is an operating checklist, not contract language.

## Economics without false precision

Use supplied numbers and make the calculation basis explicit. If inputs are missing, provide the model with blanks or clearly labeled hypothetical scenarios.

- Supplier contribution from a referred sale = collected product revenue minus referral payout, incremental delivery/support cost, and incremental selling cost.
- Reseller contribution = collected resale revenue minus supplier cost and incremental selling/delivery/support costs.
- Services contribution = services revenue minus delivery cost; keep product resale contribution separate before combining them.

State the period, currency, one-time versus recurring amounts, payout basis, and whether figures are bookings or collections. Check for double counting where supplied costs already include a payout or support charge. These are planning estimates, not audited accounting measures. Never infer quotas or commission policies from margin calculations.

## When a partnership stalls

| Observed behavior | Hypothesis to test | Useful next experiment |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor attends meetings; sellers do nothing | No seller incentive or account relevance | Work through one seller's real account and confirm credit treatment |
| Lots of account overlap; few introductions | Relationship access or customer permission is weak | Validate one relationship and ask the owner whether a specific introduction is appropriate |
| Introductions arrive; no discovery follows | Poor qualification or unclear follow-up ownership | Review recent referrals and agree an acceptance/handoff process |
| Discovery happens; opportunities stall | Weak customer case, technical gap, or commercial friction | Identify the customer's missing decision and assign the relevant owner |
| Product sells; partner disengages | Delivery burden, weak economics, or bypass concerns | Review one deal's effort, realized economics, and relationship handling |

Treat these as hypotheses. Diagnose from actual examples before recommending training, incentives, or program changes.
