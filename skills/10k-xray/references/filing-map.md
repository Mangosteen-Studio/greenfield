# Filing map: where sellers find signal in a 10-K

A 10-K is long, but a seller only needs a few sections. Read these in order.

## Item 1 — Business

What the company does, how it is organized, its customers, products, competitors and employees.

Extract:
- Reportable segments and what each one sells.
- Named strategies, programs and initiatives (“our strategy is…”, “we are focused on…”).
- Human capital statements: headcount, hiring focus, workforce changes.
- Technology, operations or supply chain descriptions that touch the seller's category.

## Item 1A — Risk Factors

What could hurt the business. Much of it is legal boilerplate repeated across companies and years.

Treat as strong signal:
- Risks described in company-specific detail (named systems, regions, programs, incidents).
- Risks that are new or rewritten compared with last year's 10-K.
- Risks that describe something already happening, not just something possible.

Treat as weak signal:
- Generic statements that would fit any company in the industry.

Never present a risk factor to a buyer as their confirmed pain. Use it to choose a discovery question.

## Item 1C — Cybersecurity

Present in recent 10-Ks. Describes how cybersecurity risk is managed and governed, often naming the responsible role. Useful for security, IT and compliance sellers.

## Item 7 — Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)

Management's explanation of results and outlook. Usually the richest source of priorities.

Extract:
- Why revenue or margins changed, in management's words.
- Cost programs, restructuring, efficiency initiatives.
- Capital expenditure plans and what they are for.
- Investments in technology, capacity, markets or acquisitions.
- Forward-looking statements (“we expect”, “we plan”, “we anticipate”).

## Segment note — Notes to the financial statements

Usually titled “Segment Information” or “Segment Reporting”. Gives revenue (and often operating income) per segment. Copy figures exactly, with units and fiscal period.

## Executive officers

Listed in Part I (often right after Item 4, titled “Information about our Executive Officers”) or incorporated by reference from the proxy statement (DEF 14A). Use these for owner names and titles.

## Proxy statement (DEF 14A), optional

Shows how executives are paid, including the metrics their bonuses depend on. Useful for understanding what leadership is measured on. It is not evidence of who buys what.

## Fresher sources

A 10-K can be up to a year old. If the user needs newer information, check the latest 10-Q (quarterly) and 8-K filings (material events), and say which filing each fact came from.

## Foreign issuers

20-F: Item 4 (Information on the Company) maps to Item 1; Item 3.D (Risk Factors) to Item 1A; Item 5 (Operating and Financial Review) to Item 7.
