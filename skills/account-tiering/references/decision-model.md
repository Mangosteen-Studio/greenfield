# Tiering contract and calibration

## Define before ranking

Write down the product, eligible account population, horizon, objective, coverage-tier meaning, hard exclusions and sources available. Name the evidence that distinguishes tiers in this particular motion. If leadership's official tiers reflect market segment rather than effort, preserve them and add a separate recommended coverage field.

A workable contract uses:
- Fit: confirmed use-case compatibility, plausible fit needing discovery, or evidenced mismatch.
- Addressable value: verified baseline and credible incremental scope, with explicit assumptions; not total company revenue.
- Ability to progress: buyer access, delivery constraints, commercial feasibility and resources.
- Sustained coverage rationale: the benefit of recurring investment versus selective development.
- Near-term action rationale: a buyer decision, risk, time-bound event or high-value information gap.

Do not multiply ordinal labels to create monetary expected value. If modeled expected return is useful, require a common horizon, incremental amounts, defensible probabilities and effort estimates. Show sensitivity and retain obligations as constraints, not just items a revenue formula can discard.

## Calibrate against contrasting accounts

Use a few representative supplied accounts before applying a newly proposed model broadly: one strong fit without urgency, one active but poor fit, one healthy customer with evidenced whitespace, one at-risk customer, and one with critical unknowns if those cases exist. Do not invent them as real accounts. Ask which disagreements would materially change the model; continue with provisional definitions where appropriate.

Check ranking for proxies: large-company bias, rewarding missing data with guessed averages, penalizing small but reachable opportunities, overweighting content engagement, and counting correlated news several times. A funding announcement repeated across five outlets is one event.

For optional numerical models, expose:
1. Each criterion and its operational definition.
2. Score scale, weights, threshold and hard gates.
3. Missing-data handling and evidence coverage independently of score.
4. Whether results are calibrated using comparable historical outcomes or merely proposed.
5. Borderline accounts and rank reversals under plausible changes.

Never impose an arbitrary distribution such as 10% Tier 1 without capacity and strategy justification. When several accounts qualify for focused coverage but capacity is scarce, preserve that demand and choose the current focus queue explicitly. A temporary scheduling constraint does not change structural fit.

## Capacity example — illustrative only

Suppose a seller has 20 account-working hours for the week: 6 hours of existing customer/deal obligations, 2 hours reserved for uncertainty and coordination, and 12 discretionary hours. Candidate tasks require 5, 4, 3 and 2 hours. All four consume 14, so they cannot all fit. Choose a defensible subset, shorten a task with its scope change visible, or defer work. Show the 2-hour overflow rather than announcing four top priorities as a feasible plan.

This is arithmetic for one fictional situation, not a recommended time split or account cadence. If an urgent obligation arrives, show the task it displaces and the consequence; do not silently expand the week.

## Decision examples — fictional, not benchmarks

| Situation | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Large prospect, strong fit, no current project or access | May justify high coverage potential; bounded research or monitoring now, not assumed purchase intent |
| Small customer, urgent support failure before renewal, little whitespace | Immediate protect/repair action can outrank prospecting without inventing expansion upside |
| Two records share a parent and domain but different procurement | Identity review; retain distinct buying centers until evidence supports a merge |
| Prospect lacks public signals because it is poorly documented | Unknown confidence and a scoped validation task; absence of news is not a negative trigger |
| Current customer has a renewal plus an expansion opportunity | One account, two workstreams; distinguish protected baseline from incremental upside |
| High score but product cannot meet a mandatory requirement | Hard constraint governs; either validate the requirement or exclude the motion with evidence |

## Re-tiering

Promote/demote when the underlying coverage case changes. A new project or accepted partner introduction may change current priority before it changes sustained coverage. Keep previous state and reason; set a review trigger that can actually be observed. Do not use CRM activity completion as proof that a blocker is resolved.
